Background

What I focus on and where that comes from.

I'm an aspiring data analyst currently completing my B.Tech in Computer Science, after a diploma in Information Technology. My interest is in the point where raw data becomes a decision — cleaning a messy export, writing the SQL to answer a specific question, then building a dashboard someone will actually open again next week.

Most of my hands-on practice has come from self-directed projects: a sales dashboard that mirrors how a small business would track performance, a placement portal that manages real student-and-company workflows, and a voice-driven assistant that pushed me into Python beyond data work. I like projects with a visible before-and-after.

I'm early in my career, so I've built my skills the way most analysts do at this stage: by picking a realistic business problem, then working through the full pipeline myself — source data, SQL, cleaning, and a dashboard at the end — rather than only practicing one step in isolation. That's shaped how I think about analysis: a chart is only useful if it's built on data you trust and answers a question someone actually asked.
